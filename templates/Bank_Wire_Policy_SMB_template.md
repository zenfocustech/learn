# Banking & Wire Policy (Small Team Edition)

**[Company Name]** | Effective: [Date] | Owner: [Name/Title] | Review: every 12 months

> **How to use this template:** Replace everything in [brackets] and delete what doesn't apply. Aim for two pages. Everyone who touches money signs it.

## 1. Purpose

Wire fraud works because one person can be rushed, tricked, or impersonated. This policy makes sure no single email, phone call, or person can move our money.

## 2. Who This Covers

Anyone who prepares, approves, or can see our bank accounts: [Owner], [Bookkeeper/Office Manager], [Other], and any outside accountant or contractor with access.

## 3. The Five Rules

1. **Two sets of eyes on every wire.** One person prepares it. A different person approves it. If only one person is available, the **owner** must approve.
2. **Call before you pay.** Confirm any new payee, or any change in bank details, by phone using a number **we already have**. Never use the number in the email or on the invoice.
3. **Urgency is a warning sign.** If someone is pressuring you, secrecy included, slow down and verify.
4. **Never share logins or codes.** Not with coworkers, the "bank," or "IT support."
5. **Report fast.** A false alarm costs nothing. A late report can cost everything.

## 5. Wire Procedure

1. **Get it in writing** (invoice, contract, or purchase order).
2. **Call to verify** the payee and bank details at a known number. Note the date, who you spoke to, and the number.
3. **Prepare** the wire. Do not release it.
4. **Approve.** The second person checks the wire against the paperwork and the call notes, then releases it.
5. **File** the paperwork and call notes in [location].
6. **Confirm** receipt with the payee.

**Approval limits:**

- Any wire: two people, with call verification.
- Payments over $[X] (any method): [Owner] approval.

## 6. Changing Vendor Bank Details

This is the most common scam. **Do not change bank details based on an email, letter, or voicemail.** Call the vendor at the known number, confirm with someone you know, and have a second person approve the change. Wait [24–48 hours] before the first payment to new details unless the owner approves otherwise.

## 7. Red Flags: Stop and Call

- "Keep this confidential" or "skip the usual process"
- New bank details or a sudden change in how we pay
- An email address that is slightly off (`acme-co.com` vs. `acmeco.com`)
- An executive who "can't talk" but needs money sent now
- Requests for gift cards, crypto, or personal accounts
- Anyone asking for a login or one-time code

## 8. Protect the Accounts

- Each person has their own banking login. No sharing.
- **Multi-factor authentication** on all bank and email accounts.
- Use a password manager. Bank only from company-approved, updated computers, never on public Wi-Fi.
- Remove access the same day someone leaves.
- Ask our bank to turn on **dual approval, wire limits, ACH debit blocks, and transaction alerts** to [email/phone].

## 9. Watch the Money

- Review bank activity every [week].
- Reconcile each account monthly.
- Report anything unfamiliar to the bank and [Owner] **the same day**.

## 10. If Something Goes Wrong

Move fast. Wire recalls are most successful in the first hours.

1. **Call the bank now:** [Name, phone, 24/7 line]. Ask for a wire recall.
2. **Tell** [Owner].
3. **Report** to the FBI at **ic3.gov** and local police.
4. **Notify** our cyber/crime insurance carrier: [Carrier, phone, policy #].
5. **Secure** accounts: change passwords and check email for hidden forwarding rules. Call [IT provider].

## 11. Review and Exceptions

We review this policy every year and when our bank, staff, or systems change. Any exception must be approved **in writing by [Owner]**, and no exception waives call verification. This policy applies to everyone, owner included.
