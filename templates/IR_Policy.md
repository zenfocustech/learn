## 1. Purpose

[Company Name] depends on its technology and data to serve customers and run the business. Security incidents will happen. This policy makes sure we respond quickly, limit the damage, meet our legal and contractual duties, and learn from each event.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, owners, contractors, and third parties who use or manage [Company Name] systems, accounts, or data. It covers all company-owned and company-managed systems, including cloud services, laptops, phones, networks, and paper records.

## 3. Definitions

- **Event:** Any observable occurrence involving our systems or data, such as a failed login or a spam email.
- **Security Incident:** An event that actually or potentially harms the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of our systems or data. Examples include ransomware, a compromised account, a lost or stolen device, unauthorized access, and data sent to the wrong party.
- **Incident Response Plan (IR Plan):** The separate document that describes the steps, contacts, and tools we use when an incident occurs.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

|Role|Assigned To|Responsibility|
|---|---|---|
|**Executive Sponsor**|[Owner/CEO]|Owns this policy. Provides resources and authority. Makes final business decisions during a major incident.|
|**Incident Response Lead**|[Name/Title]|Runs the response. Maintains the IR Plan. Decides when an event is declared an incident. Keeps the incident log.|
|**Backup Lead**|[Name/Title]|Acts when the IR Lead is unavailable.|
|**IT / Security Provider**|[Internal IT or MSP/MSSP name]|Provides technical investigation, containment, and recovery support.|
|**Communications Contact**|[Name/Title]|Handles all communication with customers, media, and the public.|

Outside parties such as legal counsel, our cyber insurance carrier, and law enforcement are engaged through the IR Lead or Executive Sponsor only.

## 5. Policy Statements

**5.1 Incident Response Plan.** [Company Name] will create, maintain, and follow a written IR Plan. The plan will define, at minimum:

- Roles and decision-making authority
- How incidents are detected, classified, and prioritized
- Steps for containment, eradication, and recovery
- Who to call, including leadership, IT/security providers, legal counsel, insurance, and law enforcement, with current contact information
- Internal and external communication rules
- Evidence preservation and documentation requirements

**5.2 Reporting.** All personnel must report suspected or confirmed security incidents to [IR Lead contact: email/phone] **immediately**, and no later than [one hour / same business day] after discovery. No one will be punished for reporting in good faith, including reporting their own mistakes.

**5.3 Response.** The IR Lead will assess every report, decide whether it is an incident, and direct the response using the IR Plan. Personnel must not delete files, wipe devices, or attempt their own fix unless instructed to.

**5.4 Documentation.** Every incident will be logged. The log will record what happened, when it was found, who was involved, what actions were taken, and the outcome.

**5.5 Legal, Regulatory, and Contractual Notification.** The Executive Sponsor, with legal counsel as needed, will decide whether the incident requires notification to customers, regulators, insurers, or other parties. This includes state breach notification laws and customer contract terms. Only the Communications Contact or Executive Sponsor may make external statements.

**5.6 Third Parties.** Vendors and service providers that handle our systems or data must be required, by contract where possible, to notify us promptly of incidents that affect us.

**5.7 Training and Awareness.** All personnel will receive security awareness training that covers how to recognize and report incidents, at hire and at least annually. Personnel with response duties will be trained on their specific roles.

**5.8 Testing.** The IR Plan will be tested at least [annually] through a tabletop exercise or similar walkthrough. Gaps found will be assigned an owner and a due date.

**5.9 Post-Incident Review.** After every significant incident, and after each test, the IR Lead will hold a lessons-learned review within [30] days. Findings will be used to update the IR Plan, security controls, and training.

**5.10 Review and Maintenance.** The Executive Sponsor will review this policy, and the IR Lead will review the IR Plan and contact list, at least annually. They will also review them after any significant incident or major change to the business or technology.

## 6. Compliance

Failure to follow this policy, including failing to report an incident, may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination or contract termination. Exceptions require written approval from the Executive Sponsor.
